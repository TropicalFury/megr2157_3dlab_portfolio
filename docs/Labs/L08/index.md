# A8 – Design & Print Something Useful

## Prompt
The objective of this assignment was to create something useful that can be 3D printed. It could be for a set purpose to the designer or for use by others. The problem could be something that troubles the designer or something that others are noticed having difficulty with that could be solved with a printed part.

The first step in the list of tasks to do was read the following article and respond to that reading with a paragraph answering this question: why does asking smart questions matter?
 - Article:  Great Questions Lead to Great Design: A Guide to the Design-thinking Process (https://www.toptal.com/designers/product-design/design-thinking-great-questions)
 - The start for any great designer is to ask questions and beyond that, asking smart questions that inform them of how something should be designed. Whether the profession is engineering, architecture, carpentry, or custom fabrication, designers do not always ask enough questions or the right ones because the environment around them demands speed and self-sufficiency. This problem may also be due to improper training, lack of confidence, or unwillingness to bother others or be seem like they do not have the necessary knowledge from the start to make a design. These are obstacles that need to be overcome because the basis of asking smarter questions is to reveal more information about a problem and how a design to remedy it should go. Good questions invite the challenge of how to answer them as opposed to ones that do not require much thought to solve. Closed questions like the standard who, what, when, where, why, and how can be important to set a structure for the overall solution but tend to stick to facts and have simple answers. Smarter questions have proper framing that compliment the subject matter being investigated and how it explores, reflects, probes, analyzes, or clears the table, to name a few examples. Smarter questions also invite questions about to set them up properly. Is the correct person being asked? Is this questions empowering discovery and learning or bringing the mood of the designer and targets down? Is it the right time or situation to ask? Answering those first can lead to the right mix of thought, delivery, and ability to reveal information that may not be thought necessary upon first inspection of a problem.

## Research & Brainstorming
**Stating the Problem and Purpose**

Drawing on the previous lab's design of a linkage for a platform with multiple height settings by way of a scissor-style lift mechanism, the goal set forth for Lab 8 was to finish out the rest of that proposed design. The linkage was already designed, documented, and printed so only the other parts needed to go through the design process. This started with some new baseline sketches and thoughts of how the multi-height setup might work. 

**Hand Sketches**

The initial thought for the base plate was to have a static set of slots that the linkages could be inserted into to set the height of the platform. This was shut down by the realization that if the walls of slots were too high the existing linkage design might not fully set in at shorter heights, and so a different mechanism was needed. The next thought was to have a cart one of the linkage ends would be put in that could slide back and forth along the base plate with something to secure it in place for a desired height setting. The two holes on the ends are drawn in for this purpose.

## Printing
All parts for this assignment were printed using white PLA filament on Prusa CORE ONE FDM 3D printers. Machines 15 and 18 were used for the printing.

Not wanting to waste time and filament on printing a full size prototype of the design, critical sections were tried first to find out if iteration was necessary to get the desired performance. This began with the sliding fork and cart and the fitment between the holes in the cart and the pins of the fork.

<img width="5712" height="4284" alt="IMG_7928" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8fa1a818-3e15-4995-9f6c-d32ae2df98dc" />

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/61e85875-56ac-42ac-a6d4-cb2ce860fa14

The first iterations of the cart and sliding fork are shown above. Upon completion and testing, including the setting of the linkage ends, the cart performed nearly as intended. It held the linkage end in place, the primary purpose of it for this test, but there was some collision between the top of the hole not connected to the linkage wall that did not allow for the lowest intended height setting to be achieved where the holes of the linkages would be 5 inches apart when measured from their respective centers. This was addressed later in the printing process under the view that this was not a highly important for the design's functionality. As for the sliding fork, the pins were too wide and too tall to fit in the cart's holes and required a new iteration. This decision also came from the urge to save time by changing fewer features of the part and less required printing time as opposed to a new iteration of the cart's holes. The second iteration was successful, providing a secure fit without side-to-side play while still allowing free sliding movement when inserting and removing the pins from the holes in the cart.

With the cart and pin fits tested and proven sufficient, the next move was to print the larger pieces, those being the bottom and top plates.

The final part was the top plate which also only needed one iteration of design. The bottom and top plates were printed simultaneously on two printers to save time rather than put them on the same printer and basically double the time to finish.

When the top and bottom plates had their printing processes started, the second version of the cart was started on another printer to try and remedy the collision mentioned between the linkage and holes of the cart. This change in the feature saved a small bit of time and finished well before the plates did. While the amount of lowering was increased for the linkage with the second iteration, there was still a collision between the top of the hole on the cart and the linkage, and it was then decided not necessary to continue to iterate for this part due to the lower amount of importance placed on this use feature for the design.
